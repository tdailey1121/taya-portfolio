Track Record: Eight Years of My Music Listening

A personal analytics project on my own Apple Music history, August 2018 to October 2026: what I listen to, when, what I skip, and whether shuffle changes how I listen.

Open the live dashboard Back to all projects

Project overview

Music is one of my favorite things, so I wanted a project where I was both the analyst and the stakeholder. I requested my full play history through Apple's Data & Privacy export and turned eight years of raw play events into a four-section interactive dashboard: Overview, Listening Habits, Skips & Loyalty, and Life Timeline.

Personal data is messy in the same ways business data is: inconsistent IDs, missing attributes, ambiguous events. Working through it end to end let me practice the full workflow on data I know well enough to sanity-check every number.

How it was built

About the AI-assisted part. I designed the data pipeline, the metric definitions and the analysis questions. The interactive web version was built with Claude (Anthropic's AI assistant) from my cleaned data, and I checked its figures against the pipeline output. The full Power BI version is in progress and will be added here when it's done.
  • Extract: Apple Music play history from Apple's Data & Privacy export.
  • Clean (Python): standardized timestamps to local time, defined what counts as a play, and filtered out progress-bar scrubs so they don't register as song endings.
  • Enrich: matched songs to artists, albums and genres through the iTunes Search API.
  • Model: organized the data into a star schema (play facts with song, artist, date and source dimensions).
  • Visualize: interactive dashboard with a year filter across every page.

Metric definitions

Getting the definitions right mattered more than any chart, so they are stated on the dashboard itself:

  • Counted play: a song listened to for 30 seconds or more (a common streaming industry convention).
  • Skip rate: skips divided by real song endings. Dragging the progress bar is not treated as an ending.
  • Artist coverage: share of listening time with a matched artist, so you can see how complete the artist rankings are.
  • Partial years: 2018 starts in August and 2026 ends in October; both are flagged wherever they appear.

The A/B question: does shuffle make me skip more?

The Skips & Loyalty page compares skip rates with shuffle on versus off, treating each as a test arm. It uses a two-proportion z-test and shows each arm's 95% confidence interval.

Because this compares my past behavior rather than a randomized experiment, the dashboard reports it as an association, not proof that shuffle causes skips. Knowing that limit, and saying so, is part of the analysis.

What the dashboard covers

Overview

Hours, counted plays, unique songs and artists, skip rate, hours by year, and top artists and songs.

Listening Habits

A weekday-by-hour heatmap of when I listen, plus hour-of-day and day-of-week breakdowns.

Skips & Loyalty

The shuffle test, skip rate by where I pressed play (album, playlist, radio), most-skipped and always-finished songs, and artists who stayed in my top 15 across years.

Life Timeline

Every month since 2018, my artist of each year, and how my genre mix shifted over time.

Data quality & limitations

  • Rarely played long-tail songs were not looked up in iTunes, so they count toward hours and plays but not toward artist or genre rankings.
  • Apple did not record shuffle state in every year, so the shuffle comparison only uses years where it exists.
  • This is one person's listening history, so findings describe my habits, not listeners in general.
