Detroit Shock Fan Lifecycle & CRM

A three-page Power BI dashboard that turns fan, engagement, ticketing and merchandise data into lifecycle stages, behavioral segments and a next-best action for every fan.

Step 01 · Source

Where the data came from

Four datasets for the same fans: profiles, engagement, ticket purchases and merchandise. This project expands my earlier Detroit Shock segmentation work.

fansengagementticket purchasesmerchandise
Step 02 · What was messy

What I had to deal with

  • Missing transaction dates: some fans had ticket or merchandise spend but no last purchase date.
  • Channel ambiguity: some fans had merchandise spend but no team shop visits, which suggests online purchases or inconsistent visit tracking.
  • No recorded activity: some fans had no email, ticket or merchandise activity at all.
  • Historical skew: some fans hadn’t been active in more than 1,000 days.
  • No acquisition or join date, so “new” fans couldn’t be identified.
Step 03 · Applied steps

How I cleaned and modeled it

  • Related all four datasets on fan_id and built a unified Fan_Master table.
  • Flagged missing dates with Missing_Ticket_Date_Flag and Missing_Merch_Date_Flag.
  • Set Days_Since_Last_Activity to −1 for fans with no activity and treated them as dormant.
  • Adjusted lifecycle thresholds to fit the historical data instead of changing dates.
  • Wrote DAX for fan value, engagement score, recency, lifecycle stage, segment and next-best action.
Step 04 · The question

What the dashboard needed to answer

Leadership needs to know which fans are loyal, which are slipping away, which have conversion potential, and what to do about each group.

Step 05 · What it shows

What the data showed

  • Most fans are in the casual segment, which is a large conversion opportunity.
  • At-risk fans are a meaningful share of the base and should be prioritized for retention.
  • At-risk high-value fans are a small but important audience for VIP retention.
  • Most recommended actions are introductory offers, reflecting the large casual base.
  • The fan-level table ties each fan’s attributes directly to a recommended action.

Step 06 · What I’d recommend

What I’d tell the business

  • Launch introductory ticket or merchandise offers to move casual fans up.
  • Re-engage at-risk fans before they go fully dormant.
  • Use app and email campaigns to turn digital fans into buyers.
  • Protect VIP and loyal fans with exclusive perks and early access.
Step 07 · Data quality & limits

What to keep in mind

  • Lifecycle stages were based on observed recency, engagement and value patterns.
  • The “New” stage was left out because the data has no join date.
  • Next-best actions are rule-based, built to demonstrate CRM decision logic.
Step 08 · What I’d do next

Where I’d take it next

  • Publish as a Power BI App.
  • Add campaign response data and a campaign ROI page.
  • Simulate revenue impact by action type.
  • Add more advanced churn-risk scoring.
