Track Record: Eight Years of My Music Listening

My own Apple Music history from August 2018 to October 2026, turned into an interactive dashboard: what I listen to, when, what I skip, and whether shuffle changes how I listen.

Step 01 · Source

Where the data came from

My full play history from Apple’s Data & Privacy export, about eight years of individual play events.

Apple Music play history
Step 02 · What was messy

What I had to deal with

  • Dragging the progress bar was logged as a song ending, which would inflate skips.
  • Rarely played songs came with no artist or genre.
  • Timestamps weren’t in local time.
  • 2018 starts in August and 2026 ends in October, so both years are partial.
Step 03 · Applied steps

How I cleaned and modeled it

  • Cleaned the export in Python and converted timestamps to local time.
  • Counted a play only at 30 seconds or more, and filtered out progress-bar scrubs.
  • Matched songs to artists, albums and genres through the iTunes Search API.
  • Modeled a star schema: play facts with song, artist, date and source dimensions.
  • Defined skip rate as skips divided by real song endings, and flagged partial years everywhere they appear.

About the AI-assisted part

I designed the pipeline, metric definitions and analysis questions. The interactive web version was built with Claude, an AI assistant, from my cleaned data, and I checked its figures against the pipeline output.

Step 04 · The question

What the dashboard needed to answer

Music is one of my favorite things, so I wanted a project where I was both the analyst and the stakeholder. What do I actually listen to, when, and does shuffle make me skip more?

Step 05 · What it shows

What the data showed

  • I’ve played 7,252 hours of music since 2018. My biggest year was 2020, with 1,076 hours.
  • That’s 142,156 counted plays across 18,422 unique songs and 1,358 artists.
  • My overall skip rate is 18.6% of real song endings.
  • The Skips & Loyalty page compares shuffle on and off with a two-proportion z-test and 95% confidence intervals.
Step 06 · Data quality & limits

What to keep in mind

  • This compares my past behavior rather than a randomized experiment, so the shuffle result is an association, not proof that shuffle causes skips.
  • Songs I rarely played weren’t looked up in iTunes, so they count toward hours and plays but not toward artist or genre rankings (artist coverage is 91.5%).
  • Apple didn’t record shuffle state in every year, so the shuffle comparison only uses years where it exists.
  • It’s one person’s listening, so it describes my habits, not listeners in general.
  • Artist and song names are left out of the public dashboard on purpose. The analysis is about listening behavior, not taste.
