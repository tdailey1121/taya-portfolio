Step 01 · Source

Where the data came from

A synthetic healthcare dataset of more than 55,000 patient records, covering admissions, diagnoses, treatment, billing and stays.

healthcare dataset · 55,000+ rows
Step 02 · What was messy

What I had to deal with

  • Raw fields like age, length of stay and billing amounts needed grouping before they could be compared.
  • Many variables were intentionally balanced, so differences between patient groups were muted.
  • The data is a snapshot in time, with no patient histories or post-discharge outcomes.
Step 03 · Applied steps

How I cleaned and modeled it

  • Cleaned and transformed the dataset in Power Query.
  • Created Patient ID, Admission Year, Age Group, Length of Stay categories and Billing categories.
  • Built DAX measures for KPI reporting and trend analysis.
  • Designed four pages: admissions, diagnoses, cost and utilization, and patient exploration.
  • Added slicers, conditional formatting and interactive filtering.
Step 04 · The question

What the dashboard needed to answer

Healthcare organizations need visibility into patient populations, admission patterns, diagnostic outcomes, treatment costs and resource use. How can one report help stakeholders spot trends in all of it?

Step 05 · What it shows

What the data showed

  • Long-stay patients were the largest utilization category.
  • Most patients fell into moderate and high-cost categories.
  • Admissions stayed relatively consistent through the year, and patient populations were balanced across major conditions.
  • Utilization metrics told a stronger story than patient volume alone.
  • The Patient Explorer lets users filter records across multiple healthcare dimensions on their own.

Step 06 · What I’d recommend

What I’d tell the business

  • Monitor utilization trends regularly.
  • Keep tracking treatment cost distributions.
  • Expand future analysis with more clinical variables.
  • Use interactive dashboards to support operational planning.
Step 07 · Data quality & limits

What to keep in mind

  • The data is synthetic, not real patients or healthcare organizations.
  • Because variables were intentionally balanced, the analysis leaned on utilization, cost, admissions and operational trends rather than dramatic group differences.
  • There are no complete patient histories or post-discharge outcomes.
Step 08 · What I’d do next

Where I’d take it next

  • Integrate real-world healthcare datasets.
  • Add patient outcome metrics.
  • Build predictive models for utilization.
  • Publish as a Power BI App for broader stakeholder access.
